Anatomy of a mobile robot (3D)

Turn the robot with the mouse or your finger. Click an axis (a coloured circle or a card) to light up its ring and its axis, press X-ray to look inside at the motors, gearboxes, controller and cables, and move each joint with its slider.

A gear motor turns the left wheel. The slider sets its speed (positive = forward). Its encoder measures the real speed.

Same on the right. If the right wheel is faster, the robot turns left; with opposite speeds it spins on the spot.

A free ball or swivel wheel with no motor: a third support point so the robot does not tip over.

The lidar (a rotating laser) measures distances all around to see obstacles and build a map. The front bumper and an IMU (gyroscope) complete the sensors.

The heaviest part: it sits low, between the wheels, to keep the robot stable. Its capacity sets the autonomy. Seen in X-ray.

A microcontroller runs the fast loops (PID on the wheel speeds, using the encoders) and a small computer runs navigation and mapping. Seen in X-ray.

What is inside each joint?

  • Two gear motors with encoders: the encoders measure each wheel speed for the PID loops and for the odometry (which drifts, see the course on perception).
  • Caster: a free wheel, the third point of support.
  • Battery placed low, and motor drivers (H-bridges) that turn the controller commands into motor current.
  • Small computer (single-board) for navigation, mapping and communication, for example with ROS 2.
  • Sensors: lidar for obstacles and maps, bumpers and cliff sensors for safety, IMU for the heading.

The wheel speeds are in rad/s: v = r (ωL + ωR) / 2 and ω = r (ωR − ωL) / L, with r = 0.07 m and L = 0.40 m as on the mobile robot page. The orange line is the path the robot has driven (odometry). The model is simplified: the real inside depends on the make.
