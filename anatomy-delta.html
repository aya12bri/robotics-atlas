Definition

A Delta robot is a parallel robot: three arms driven by motors fixed on the base are linked to one small platform by parallelograms, which keep the platform always horizontal. Because the motors do not move, the moving parts are very light and the robot is extremely fast. It is used for high-speed pick and place of light objects (food, pharmacy, electronics).

The motor, fixed on the top plate, swings the upper arm up and down. All three motors are fixed on the base: the moving parts are very light, which is why the Delta is so fast.

Same, at 240°. When the three motors turn together by the same angle, the effector goes straight up or down.

Two parallel rods per arm, with ball joints at both ends. They keep the effector parallel to the top plate: it never tilts. These joints have no motor.

The small moving platform with the tool (here a suction cup). It has 3 degrees of freedom: x, y and z. An optional fourth motor in the centre can turn the tool.

What is inside each joint?

  • Three servo motors with gearboxes on the top plate: they are fixed, so only the light arms move.
  • Carbon-fibre arms and rods: very light, so accelerations of several g are possible and more than 100 picks per minute.
  • Ball joints at the ends of the rods, held by springs: no play, so the effector stays rigid.
  • Central telescopic shaft (optional, seen in X-ray): a fourth axis that turns the tool, driven by a motor on the top plate.
  • Controller and camera: usually a vision system above the conveyor tells the robot where each product is.

The motor angles θ are measured as on the Delta page: 0° when the upper arm is horizontal, positive downward. The effector position is computed live from the three angles (forward kinematics). The model is simplified: the real inside depends on the make.
