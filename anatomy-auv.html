Definition

An underwater robot (AUV, autonomous underwater vehicle) is a torpedo-shaped robot that dives alone, without a cable or a pilot. A propeller pushes it, fins steer it, and it finds its way without GPS with sonars and an inertial unit. It maps the sea floor and inspects pipelines and wrecks.

Pilot commands

Set the propeller thrust, then turn with the rudders and dive or climb with the elevators.

A propeller in a duct, turned by a brushless motor sealed in oil or behind a magnetic coupling, so that the water never reaches the electronics.

Four fins at the tail: the two vertical ones (rudders) turn the vehicle, the two horizontal ones (elevators) tilt the nose to dive or climb.

A tube of aluminium or titanium that resists the pressure of the water: 1 bar more every 10 m, so 300 bar at 3 000 m. Seen in X-ray.

There is no GPS under water: a DVL (four sonar beams to the sea floor) measures the speed, an IMU the turns, a pressure sensor the depth; the side-scan sonar draws a map of the floor.

Lithium batteries for 10 to 24 hours of mission, a computer that follows the mission plan alone, and foam blocks that make the vehicle float. Seen in X-ray.

At the surface, the antenna gets a GPS position and sends the data by radio; under water, only sound carries messages (an acoustic modem). The flashing light helps to find it.

Kinematics: steering in water

An underwater vehicle moves in 6 directions: surge (forward), sway (sideways), heave (up and down), roll, pitch and yaw. A torpedo-shaped AUV is like a plane: one propeller pushes it forward, and its fins turn the water flow into forces. It can only steer while it moves, and it cannot go sideways on its own.

Buoyancy and depth

An AUV is made a little lighter than water (slightly positive buoyancy): if the power fails, it floats back to the surface. To stay deep it uses its elevators while it moves; some gliders change their volume instead.

What is inside each joint?

  • No cable, no pilot: an AUV (autonomous underwater vehicle) follows its mission alone, unlike an ROV, which is driven by a pilot through a cable from a ship.
  • Navigation without GPS: it adds up the speed of the DVL and the turns of the IMU (dead reckoning, corrected by a Kalman filter), and comes back to the surface now and then to get a GPS fix.
  • Uses: mapping the sea floor, inspecting pipelines and cables, searching for wrecks, studying the ocean.

The underwater robot really moves: the propeller pushes it forward, the rudders turn it and the elevators tilt its nose to dive or climb. Like a plane, the fins only work when the vehicle moves through the water. The model is simplified.

Where are underwater robots used?

The sea is dark, deep and without GPS: robots go where divers cannot, for hours or weeks.
