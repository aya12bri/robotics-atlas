Cylindrical R-P-P

← Robot list · Learn the basics

A column turns on its base (q1), a carriage slides up and down the column (q2), and an arm slides in and out (q3). The tool moves in cylindrical coordinates: an angle, a height and a radius. One of the six classic families of robot arms, with the Cartesian, spherical, SCARA, articulated and parallel robots.

Joints

One rotation, then two slides. Move a slider, or type a value in a number box.

Kinematic diagram (schéma cinématique)

Frames:

The circle is the revolute joint q1 (its axis is vertical), the squares are the sliding joints q2 and q3. The side view turns with q1, so the arm always lies in the picture. Axes: x red, y green, z blue. ⊙ points at you, ⊗ points away.

DH table (standard)

Frame i is attached to the axis of joint i+1. Each row is: Ai = Rz(θ) · Tz(d) · Tx(a) · Rx(α). For a sliding joint, d is the variable. Orange numbers are the current values.

DHM table (modified DH)

Frame i is attached to the axis of joint i. Each row is: Ai = Rx(αi−1) · Tx(ai−1) · Rz(θ) · Tz(d).

Result: where is the tool?


    
The last column is the position of the tool in the base frame {0}. Both tables give exactly this same matrix.


    
The models in three lines

    
Direct model (MGD):    x = q3 · cos q1     y = q3 · sin q1     z = h + q2
Inverse model (MGI):   q1 = atan2(y, x)    q3 = √(x² + y²)     q2 = z − h      (h = 0.30 m)

    
The joints are exactly the cylindrical coordinates of the tool (angle, height, radius). The inverse model has a single answer, except on the axis of the column (x = y = 0), where q1 is undefined: that line is the singularity of this robot, and the sleeve of the column keeps the tool away from it.


    
Workspace

    
    
With q3 between rmin and rmax and q2 between its two stops, the tool sweeps a hollow cylinder (a thick tube), cut by the stops of q1 if the column cannot turn all the way round. The volume is the area of the ring times the stroke of q2: V = θrange/2 · (rmax² − rmin²) · stroke.


    

      
Where is a cylindrical robot used?

      
Loading and unloading machine tools and presses, die casting and forging, handling in clean rooms (wafers, glass), stacking, and simple assembly. The Versatran (1960), one of the first industrial robots, was cylindrical; today you meet the structure in wafer handlers, in tabletop teaching robots and in many “pick and place” units.

      
Strengths: very simple maths, rigid in the vertical direction, the tool keeps its orientation, a large reach for a small footprint. Weaknesses: it cannot reach behind the column or near its base, the sliding axes need protected guides, and the arm sticks out behind when it retracts.

      
End effector (tool)

      
A gripper or a vacuum cup that points radially, or downwards with a small wrist. Add a fourth joint at the end of the arm to turn the part.
