Definition

A SCARA robot (Selective Compliance Assembly Robot Arm) has two rotary joints with vertical axes that move the tool in a horizontal plane, a vertical slide and a rotation of the tool: 4 degrees of freedom. It is stiff vertically and slightly compliant horizontally, which makes it fast and precise for assembly and pick and place from above.

Turns the whole arm around the vertical column. The motor and the gearbox are in the column.

Turns the second link around a vertical axis at the end of the first one. Together with J1 it places the tool in the horizontal plane.

A rod slides up and down through the end of link 2, driven by a ball screw. It sets the height of the tool. The only linear axis.

Spins the tool around the vertical rod: it sets the angle of the gripper in the plane.

What is inside each joint?

  • Servo motors at the base and at the elbow, with harmonic gearboxes: stiff in the plane, which is why a SCARA is so precise.
  • Ball screw and spline shaft: the end of link 2 holds two motors, one for the up-and-down motion (J3) and one for the rotation of the tool (J4), both acting on the same hollow rod.
  • Brake on J3: gravity pulls the rod down, so the brake holds it when the power is off.
  • Cables and air pass through the hollow rod to the gripper (vacuum or compressed air).
  • Controller: in the column or in a separate box.
