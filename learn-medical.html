From the surgeon's hand to the robot: a short history

25 min
You will learn

how surgery became modern (anaesthesia, antisepsis, antibiotics), how imaging and minimally invasive surgery changed it, and the main milestones of medical robots since 1985.

In one sentence

Medical robots came last, on top of anaesthesia, imaging and minimally invasive surgery: they extend the hands and the eyes of the doctor, they do not replace them.

1.1 Four steps to the robot

StepWhat changedLandmarks
modern surgeryoperating without pain, without infectionanaesthetics, antiseptics, anticoagulants, antibiotics, analgesics (19th–20th c.)
medical imagingseeing inside the bodyX-rays 1895, ultrasound 1947–49, CT scanner 1975, first MRI image of a human 1977
minimally invasive surgeryoperating through small holes with a camera1987: first video laparoscopic gallbladder removal (Mouret, Lyon)

1.2 The milestones of medical robots

In 1930 the president of the Royal College of Surgeons of England said that surgery had “almost reached its limits”. Minimally invasive surgery and robots proved him wrong — but, as the course insists, with very few commercial successes for many prototypes: a medical robot must prove a real benefit for the patient, at an acceptable cost.

What this course is about

Robots for interventional procedures and minimally invasive surgery: mostly manipulators (arms), with teleoperation, co-manipulation and imaging. Mobile robots in hospitals (logistics, disinfection) are only mentioned.

Why a robot? Keeping the doctor in the loop

In one sentence

The robot brings precision and steadiness, the doctor brings judgement: medical robotics is robotic assistance, with the practitioner in the loop.

2.1 Teleoperation: the doctor at a distance

  • Scaling of movements and forces: 3 cm of the hand can become 1 cm at the tool.
  • Richer feedback: magnified 3D image, force feedback (haptics).
  • Action at a distance or in a hostile place (radiation).
  • More degrees of freedom and dexterity: a wrist at the tip of the instrument, inside the body.
  • Filtering of tremor and compensation of physiological motion (breathing, heartbeat).

xtool = xhand / k   (scaling factor k, e.g. 3)

The physiological tremor of the hand is a small oscillation of about 8 to 12 Hz, while voluntary surgical gestures are much slower (a few hertz at most). A low-pass filter on the master signal removes the tremor and keeps the gesture.

Degrees of freedom and the fixed point of the trocar

30 min
You will learn

what degrees of freedom and the configuration space mean for a medical robot, why an instrument through a trocar keeps only 4 of them, the fulcrum effect, and the remote centre of motion (RCM) that surgical robots are built around.

In one sentence

In minimally invasive surgery the instrument must pivot about the hole in the skin: the robot is built so that this point never moves.

3.1 Degrees of freedom

In anatomy, each possible movement at a joint is a degree of freedom (DOF); a robot is the same. A 2-DOF arm (2 joints, 2 links) has a 2-dimensional configuration space (θ₁, θ₂): every point of it is one posture, and an obstacle in the room becomes a forbidden region of this space (see the singularities and the intelligence courses).

3.2 The trocar removes 2 DOF

A rigid body has 6 DOF. Through the trocar the instrument cannot move sideways at the wall: the 2 sideways translations are lost. 4 DOF remain: 2 tilts around the trocar, the insertion along the instrument, and the rotation about its own axis. To get the 6 DOF back inside the body, robots add a small wrist at the tip of the instrument (the EndoWrist of the da Vinci).

3.3 The fulcrum effect

dtip = − dhand × Lin / Lout

The trocar is a pivot: when the hand goes left the tip goes right (the minus sign), and the movement is multiplied by the ratio of the lengths inside and outside. This inversion is hard to learn; a robot computes it and gives back intuitive gestures.

The remote centre of motion (RCM)

Surgical robots keep the instrument pivoting about the trocar either mechanically (a parallelogram whose fixed point is away from the robot, as on the da Vinci arms, or a spherical mechanism) or by software (a standard arm whose controller enforces the constraint). The mechanical solution is intrinsically safe: even a bug cannot tear the abdominal wall.

Key terms

  • degree of freedom (DOF)
  • configuration space
  • trocar
  • fulcrum effect
  • remote centre of motion (RCM)

Co-manipulation: the robot and the doctor hold the tool together

25 min
You will learn

the three kinds of co-manipulation (parallel, serial, orthotic), what each one adds up, and what they bring: more force, more dexterity, more accuracy, guidance.

In one sentence

In co-manipulation the doctor keeps the tool in hand and the robot shares the gesture: it can steady it, guide it or forbid a zone.

KindWhat adds upExample
parallelthe forces on the tool: F = Fhuman + Frobota cobot arm holding a needle with the radiologist; a patient positioning table guided by hand
serialthe velocities: vtool = vhand + vrobota hand-held instrument with an active tip that cancels tremor
orthoticthe joint torques of the arman exoskeleton (rehabilitation, ReWalk)

4.1 Virtual guides

A co-manipulated robot can create virtual fixtures: a forbidden region (a nerve, an artery) that the tool cannot enter, or a guide (a line, a cone) that pulls the tool towards the planned path. The doctor still moves the tool; the robot only resists or helps — exactly the force control of the control course (impedance), with the patient's anatomy as the reference.

Link with the teleoperation course

Co-manipulation is the medical side of cobotics (teleoperation, cobotics, architectures and safety course, lessons 7 and 8): same intrinsic safety, same force sensors, same human–robot sharing.

Constraints: safety, sterility, the operating room, regulation

30 min
You will learn

what makes medical robotics harder than industrial robotics: zero accidents tolerated, sterility, the operating room, the long validation and the CE marking or FDA clearance.

In one sentence

A medical robot works next to a patient who cannot move away: safety comes first, then sterility, then the room, then the law.

5.1 Safety: zero accidents

  • a doctor in the loop, precise protocols, trained staff;
  • clear, ergonomic interfaces; automatic initialisation; a procedure to disengage the robot and finish by hand;
  • intrinsically safe architectures, mechanical and electrical fuses, redundant sensors;
  • limited workspace, speed and force; software tests and step-by-step validation of the medical procedure.

5.2 Sterility

MethodHowFor
autoclavesteam at 134 °C for 18 minmetal instruments that resist heat
hydrogen peroxide plasma (Sterrad®)low temperature, about 50 °Csome heat-sensitive devices
single use, sterile packagingthrown away after one patientdrapes covering the robot, some instruments

5.3 The operating room

Small footprint; a workspace that the staff can see and understand (a SCARA-like structure is easier to read than a 6-axis arm); a system one person can move and install; easy entry and exit; maintenance and breakdown plans; electromagnetic compatibility with the other equipment.

5.4 Validation and regulation

A medical robot is a medical device: it needs the CE marking in Europe (Medical Device Regulation 2017/745) or FDA clearance in the United States, after proving the medical benefit, the technical and economic feasibility, and the human factors. The course's honest warning: very few prototypes become products.

Four questions before starting a project
  1. Does it rely on real, high-quality medical expertise?
  2. Can the prototype become a certifiable system?
  3. Will the robot objectively improve the doctor's gesture?
  4. Is that improvement significant for the patient or the staff?

Robots in the hospital today

In one sentence

Each family of medical robots answers one limit of the human hand: three hands needed, a bone to cut exactly, a beam to aim at a moving tumour.

6.1 Why minimally invasive surgery needs help

Tele-ultrasound: an expert examines a distant patient

30 min
You will learn

how a robotic tele-ultrasound system works, the degrees of freedom the probe needs, why its mechanism is spherical, how the delay of the link is handled, and the PRISME prototypes from 1998 to the Melody robot.

In one sentence

The expert moves a dummy probe in a hospital; on the other side of the world a light robot reproduces the gesture on the patient, and the images come back.

7.1 The degrees of freedom of the probe

A sonographer keeps the probe in contact with the skin and mostly orients it: 3 rotations about the contact point, plus a translation along the probe to press more or less. The large movements over the body are done by the assistant who places the robot. That is why the PRISME robots use a spherical (parallel) mechanism whose rotations all pass through the contact point — a remote centre of motion, as in lesson 3.

DOF of the probe = 3 rotations about the contact point + 1 translation (pressure) = 4

7.2 The delay of the link

A geostationary satellite is about 36 000 km high: a signal goes up and down in about 0.24 s, and a command followed by its image takes about twice that, around half a second with the processing. With such delays a direct force loop would become unstable (the teleoperation course explains why and how wave variables fix it). Tele-ultrasound robots therefore send the gestures, control the contact force locally with the force sensor of the patient station, and send back images and a measured force.

Glossary

All the words of the course in one place.

TermMeaning
Minimally invasive surgery (MIS)Surgery through small holes with long instruments and a camera.
TrocarThe tube through the abdominal wall that instruments pass through.
Remote centre of motion (RCM)A fixed point, away from the robot, about which the tool pivots.
Fulcrum effectThrough a trocar the tip moves the opposite way to the hand, scaled by L in / L out.
TeleoperationThe doctor moves a master arm, a slave robot reproduces the gesture.
Co-manipulationThe doctor and the robot hold and move the tool together.
Virtual fixtureA forbidden zone or a guiding path created by the robot's control.
Motion scalingThe tool moves k times less than the hand.
Physiological tremorA small oscillation of the hand, around 8–12 Hz.
Medical device, CE markingThe legal status of a medical robot in Europe (Regulation 2017/745).
Tele-ultrasoundAn ultrasound examination made at a distance through a robot holding the probe.
PhantomAn artificial model of the body used to test a robot.
