Kinematic diagram (schéma cinématique), top view

Frames:

Axes: x red, y green, z blue. A circle with a dot ⊙ is an axis pointing at you, a circle with a cross ⊗ is an axis pointing away from you. The square is the sliding joint q3, seen from above.

DH table (standard)

Frame i is attached to the axis of joint i+1. Each row is one transformation: Ai = Rz(θ) · Tz(d) · Tx(a) · Rx(α). Orange numbers are the current values.

DHM table (modified DH)

Frame i is attached to the axis of joint i. Each row is: Ai = Rx(αi−1) · Tx(ai−1) · Rz(θ) · Tz(d).

Result: where is the tool?


    
The last column is the position of the tool in the base frame {0}. The 3×3 block is its
       orientation. Both tables, multiplied row by row, give exactly this same matrix.


    
Forward kinematics of a SCARA

    
x = L1·cos(q1) + L2·cos(q1+q2)
y = L1·sin(q1) + L2·sin(q1+q2)
z = 0.60 + 0.10 − (q3 + 0.30) − 0.06
tool angle = q1 + q2 − q4      (z points DOWN, so q4 turns the other way)


    

      
Where is a SCARA used?

      
Electronics factories (placing and inserting components on circuit boards), screwing
         and small-parts assembly, packaging, pick-and-place of light objects, and dispensing
         glue or sealant. It is fast (a pick-and-place in under a second) and very stiff
         vertically, but soft sideways, which helps to insert a pin into a hole.

      
End effector (tool)

      
Yes, a pinch gripper (pince) works: a two-finger parallel gripper, like
         the one drawn in the 3D model. Other tools:

      
    
        
  • Vacuum suction cup, the most common choice for flat, light parts.

  • Electric screwdriver, for screwing.
